Published November 2020, and the first interview in the series.

**Who she is.** Degorram runs a [YouTube channel](http://www.youtube.com/degorram)
of let's-play videos covering old-school shooters, including the Serpent Riders
trilogy. She is roughly the same age as the games she plays — she came to them
through watching her father play Doom as a child — which makes her a useful
counterweight to the other two interviews: not a nostalgic contemporary, but
someone who inherited these games and stayed.

## Ground the conversation covers

**Background.** Playing since the late 1990s, starting with *X-Wing* and moving
quickly to Quake and then Hexen. A stated preference for cinematic, story-driven
first-person games over puzzle-led or pure-action ones, and an admitted weakness
for a strong soundtrack — she reckons about half her listening comes from games.

**The road not taken.** Games are a hobby rather than a career. She considered
studying games writing but read English instead, worried a narrow degree would not
find work. There is a good anecdote about later getting to ask **Bruce Nesmith**,
then Bethesda's design director, how to move into the field: his advice was to get
proficient at coding and that modders using studio-released tools do get noticed.
Coding never held her interest enough, and the idea was shelved.

**Why the channel exists, and what it is not.** She started publishing at the
beginning of 2017, having wanted to since around 2009 and been unable to afford
equipment as a student. She is explicit that she is not making tutorials or
criticism: her model is channels that make playing feel like playing alongside
friends, and she names the Yogscast and Markiplier as the influences. Asked
directly about insight, she declines the premise — she will happily give an
opinion on a soundtrack or a story, but is wary of claiming grand readings of games
that have been analysed for decades.

**On the trilogy, including a dissent.** She rates Hexen the highest of the three,
arguing it cleaned up what she found chaotic in Heretic and tells a clearer story.
Her verdict on Hexen II is the interesting one, because it is not the answer a
Hexen II fan site would have chosen: it is her least favourite of the trilogy,
because in her reading the effort that went into the new graphics came at the
expense of the other elements. She is careful to add that it is still a good game
and worth playing to finish the series. Inky prints it unedited, which speaks well
of both of them.

**How the videos get made.** A practical rundown that will be useful to anyone
recording their own: OBS for game capture, audio recorded separately in Adobe
Audition through an Audio-Technica USB condenser mic, a Logitech webcam she is
frank about the age of, everything assembled in Premiere. She keeps editing
minimal on principle, on the grounds that editing for its own sake does not make a
video better.

**Video length.** On why episodes run twenty to twenty-five minutes — a compromise
between the conventional fifteen-minute attention-span target, which she finds too
short, and the longer videos she actually watches herself — and a cheerful
admission that her habitual sign-off exists because ending a video gracefully is
awkward every single time.

**Whether the puzzles are staged.** Inky asks — politely, having had his suspicions
about how quickly she solved Blackmarsh's notorious sand/glass/lens puzzle —
whether sequences are ever scripted in advance. Her answer is worth reading in
full: recording while thinking makes puzzles harder, not easier; she does sometimes
look up a solution when badly stuck and cut the aimless wandering, but tries to say
so on camera; and she offers a counter-example of an entire Quake II episode spent
hunting a key that was in plain sight. The page notes that Hexen II's most infamous
puzzle got her in the end anyway.

**Audience and isolation.** On how hard it is to build a following against
YouTube's recommendation algorithm, how little feedback she receives, and the fact
that most of her viewers arrive by searching for a specific game and already know
it.

**The modding scene.** Inky was the first person from the mapping and modding
community she had connected with, and she says the conversation prompted her to go
looking at community releases. She followed through: her playthrough of [Wheel Of
Karma](../../wheel-of-karma/) is linked from the page, and Inky's own project pages
credit her series.

**Future plans**, including *System Shock*, *Thief* and a rueful promise to attempt
*Myst* — and, at the end, the origin of her habit of calling every spider a
"spoder", which turns out to be a typo in a Christmas social media post that stuck.
